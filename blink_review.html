How to label a frame

Look at the dark pupil and its edge. We are judging whether the image can support a useful pupil measurement.

  • 1 · Usable: enough of the pupil boundary is visible and sharp to estimate its oval shape.
  • 2 · Unusable: eyelid closure, missing boundary, clipping, blur or glare prevents a credible measurement. Add a short reason.
  • 3 · Uncertain: you cannot decide confidently. It stays saved for discussion and is excluded from mistake rates.

A small bright reflection alone does not automatically make a frame unusable. Judge how much it obscures the boundary. Use nearby frames for context, but judge the selected frame itself.

What is already built?

“Built” means the code exists and has software checks. It does not mean real-world accuracy has been proven.

✓ Pupil checks and blink recovery

pupil_detection.py measures pupils, checks image evidence, and holds back frames until several strong observations indicate recovery. The pipeline keeps separate state for each eye.

✓ Protection before the 3D model

eye_pipeline.py rejects unsuitable measurements before they reach pye3d. Bad frames do not become observations in its eye model. Good eligible observations continue helping the model learn.

✓ Coordinates and diagnostic checks

calibration.py handles rotations and distortion. eye_model_estimation.py handles focal scaling, records failed model checks, and separates finite output from reliable output. Known-geometry tests cover these conversions.

✓ Review, comparison and saved reports

blink_validation.py is this workspace: independent reviewer files, resumable labels, paired real pipeline runs and scoring. The current full recordings have been processed in both modes.

✓ Calibration and gaze-validation tools

The code can fit separate camera calibrations and compare gaze directions to known references. Your own checkerboard images and known-target recordings are still required to establish physical accuracy.

Still to do: human and fresh-recording validation

Label and discuss the frames, inspect the mistakes, tune on development data, then test the frozen filter on new recordings. Camera calibration identity and gaze accuracy remain unverified for the supplied recordings.

2 · Understand the filter’s mistakes

Bad frames admitted: you marked a frame unusable, but the algorithm fed it into the 3D eye model. Fewer is better.

Good frames discarded: you marked a frame usable, but the algorithm skipped it. Fewer is better. Improving one measure can worsen the other.
